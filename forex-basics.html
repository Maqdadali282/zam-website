Forex Basics

Welcome to Your Forex Trading Journey

What is forex trading? Forex (short for Foreign Exchange) is the global marketplace where currencies are traded. It's the largest financial market in the world, with a daily trading volume of over $7 trillion. Traders buy one currency while selling another at the same time — the goal is to profit from changes in currency values.

This page walks you step by step through every core term you need before you place a single trade — in plain English, with an example and a picture for anything that's easier to understand visually than in words.

Start Here

Terms you'll see everywhere

Learn these first — almost every forex conversation and chart uses them.

Currency Pair

Forex is always traded in pairs, because you're buying one currency while selling another at the same time.

e.g. EUR/USD, GBP/JPY

Base & Quote Currency

In EUR/USD, EUR is the "base" currency (the one you're buying/selling) and USD is the "quote" currency (what it's priced in).

EUR/USD → EUR is base, USD is quote

Exchange Rate

The price of one currency expressed in terms of another — how much of the quote currency you need to buy one unit of the base currency.

EUR/USD 1.0850 = 1 EUR costs 1.0850 USD

Bid, Ask & Spread

The "bid" is the price you can sell at; the "ask" is the price you can buy at. The tiny gap between them is the spread — the broker's built-in fee.

Bid 1.0848 / Ask 1.0850 = 2 pip spread

Pip & Pipette

A pip is the smallest standard price move a pair makes — usually the 4th decimal place. A pipette is one-tenth of a pip (the 5th decimal), used for extra-precise pricing.

1.08500 → 1.08501 = 1 pipette · 1.0850 → 1.0851 = 1 pip

Lot, Standard, Mini & Micro Lot

A "lot" is the size of your trade. Standard, mini, and micro lots control exactly how much currency — and how much money — is actually on the line.

Standard = 100,000 · Mini = 10,000 · Micro = 1,000 units

Leverage

Borrowed buying power from your broker that lets you control a large position with a much smaller deposit. It multiplies both potential profit and potential loss.

100:1 leverage → $100 controls $10,000

Buy & Sell / Long & Short

"Buy" (going long) means you expect the price to rise. "Sell" (going short) means you expect it to fall — you can profit from prices dropping too.

Long = betting up · Short = betting down

Open & Close Position

"Opening" a position is entering a trade. "Closing" it is exiting — either manually, or automatically via your Stop Loss/Take Profit.

Open at 1.0850 → Close at 1.0900 = profit

Stop Loss (SL) & Take Profit (TP)

A Stop Loss automatically closes a losing trade before it gets worse. A Take Profit automatically locks in gains once price hits your target.

Protects your account while you're away from the screen
Your Trading Account

Balance, Equity & Margin

The four numbers on your account panel that actually tell you how much risk you're carrying.

How your account balance really breaks down

These four numbers move together, and understanding them stops you from getting an unexpected margin call.

  • Balance The money in your account before any open trades.
  • Equity Balance adjusted for the live profit/loss of open trades right now.
  • Used Margin The portion of your funds locked up as a deposit for open trades.
  • Free Margin Equity minus Used Margin — what's actually left to open new trades.
Reading A Chart

Candlestick anatomy

Almost every trading platform uses candlesticks by default — here's exactly what each part means.

One candle = one time period

Each candlestick shows four prices for whatever period you're viewing (1 minute, 1 hour, 1 day...): where price opened, where it closed, and the highest and lowest points it touched.

  • Body The thick part — the range between open and close.
  • Wick The thin lines — the high and low extremes for that period.
  • Green/filled Price closed higher than it opened (bullish).
  • Red/hollow Price closed lower than it opened (bearish).

Support & Resistance

These are price levels the market keeps "bouncing off." Support is a floor where buyers tend to step in; resistance is a ceiling where sellers tend to step in.

  • Support A level price struggles to fall below.
  • Resistance A level price struggles to rise above.
  • Breakout When price finally punches through one of these levels.
Placing A Trade

Market order vs pending order

How you tell your platform exactly when and how to enter a trade.

Instant

Market Order

Buy or sell immediately at the current market price. Fastest way in, but you take whatever price is available right now.

Planned

Pending Order

Set now, executes later — only once price reaches a level you choose. Splits into four types depending on direction and price level, shown below.

The 4 pending order types

Pending orders are named by where your target price sits compared to the current price.

  • Buy Limit Buy below the current price — expecting a bounce up.
  • Sell Limit Sell above the current price — expecting a drop down.
  • Buy Stop Buy above the current price — expecting a breakout up.
  • Sell Stop Sell below the current price — expecting a breakout down.
How Traders Decide

Technical vs fundamental analysis

Technical Analysis

Studying price charts, patterns, and indicators (like moving averages or RSI) to predict where price is likely headed next — based purely on price behavior itself.

Fundamental Analysis

Looking at the economic "why" behind price moves — interest rates, inflation, employment data, and central bank decisions that drive a currency's real value.

Quick Reference

A few more terms you'll run into

Volatility

How fast and how much a price moves. High volatility = bigger, faster swings (and bigger risk).

Margin Call

A warning from your broker that your account no longer has enough funds to keep a losing trade open.

Drawdown

The drop from your account's highest value down to its current value — a key measure of risk.

Demo vs Live Account

A demo account uses fake money to practice risk-free. A live account trades with real funds.

Economic Calendar

A schedule of upcoming news events (interest rate decisions, jobs reports) that tend to move the market.

Broker & Trading Platform

A broker gives you market access; the platform (like MT4/MT5) is the software you use to place trades.

Swap / Rollover

A small interest fee (or credit) applied when you hold a position open overnight.

Major, Minor & Exotic Pairs

Majors involve USD (EUR/USD); minors don't (EUR/GBP); exotics pair a major with an emerging-market currency (USD/TRY).
